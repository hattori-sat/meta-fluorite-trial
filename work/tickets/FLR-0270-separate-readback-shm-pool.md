# FLR-0270 — separate readback SHM pool from control surface

- Status: Done
- Priority: High
- Owner: readback SHM pool / compositor import
- Created: 2026-09-24
- Predecessor: [FLR-0269](FLR-0269-trace-readback-wayland-protocol.md)

## Objective

Test whether sharing one enlarged `wl_shm_pool` between the visible control
buffer and readback buffers causes the delayed whole-frame blackout. Preserve
the known-good control surface allocation and move only readback storage into
a separate pool.

## Facts

- SHM-only control on the same image displays 2D plus a self-made 3D cube.
- Readback-mode publish/release is accepted by Wayland, but QMP transitions
  from 2D-visible to full black.
- The current FLR-0267 implementation enlarges the control pool to three
  buffers and creates readback buffers at nonzero offsets in that pool.

## Hypotheses and alternatives

1. Leading: the shared enlarged pool or offset buffers create a compositor
   lifetime/import interaction. Separate pool allocation should preserve the
   control path and make the readback surface independent.
2. Alternative: the callback publish/flush ordering is causal; separate pools
   will remain black and the next boundary will be display-thread sequencing.

## Verification plan

- Make one minimal Devtool source change: keep the control pool at its original
  size and allocate a separate readback pool/mapping for the two readback
  buffers.
- Generate the patch through the recorded Devtool source commit/update-recipe
  flow, then Mini `do_patch`, compile, image, and QMP 10-frame validation.
- Acceptance requires 2D HUD plus chromatic readback ROI in live QMP frames and
  zero residual QEMU processes.

## Scope boundary

Do not change production scene, camera, light, Flutter route, or native Vulkan
WSI. Keep the SHM control and native-only paths unchanged.

## Check

- Mini `do_patch`, `do_compile`, and full image build: PASS for the exact
  `e876be0` layer revision.
- QEMU `preflight`, `start`, guest readiness, protocol launch, and teardown:
  PASS. The recorded QMP quit completed with `residual_targets=0` and
  `residual_qmp=0`.
- The first ten QMP frames in run `0289-readback-pool` were stable rather than
  transitioning to full black: full-frame SHA prefix `f686a3c2769c`,
  `598250/1024000` changed pixels, and geometry indicators present in every
  frame. This was only an early observation window.
- A follow-up run with the same image and pool separation, `0290-surface-create`,
  later produced five consecutive full-black QMP frames with SHA prefix
  `d4e96a65fd4f` and `0/1024000` changed pixels. The separate pool therefore
  does not prove a durable fix for the delayed blackout.
- The readback ROI `(460,260,360,280)` remained black in all ten frames:
  `0/100800` changed and `0/100800` chromatic. This is a visual-gate FAIL,
  not a product 3D success.
- Runtime markers prove both separate-pool readback buffers contained
  `100800/100800` nonzero and chromatic pixels, were attached/damaged/committed
  on `wl_surface@43`, and were later released. No Wayland protocol error was
  observed.

## Act

Close this diagnostic boundary as **Done: partial hypothesis result**. The
separate readback pool changes the early timing/content window, but it does not
durably remove the delayed whole-frame blackout and does not make the readback
child surface visible in QMP.
FLR-0271 owns the next boundary: identify why the accepted readback child
surface is omitted from the composed framebuffer. Do not call this patch a
Fluorite production 3D fix.
