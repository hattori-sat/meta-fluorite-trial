## Facts

- The fixed Mini receiver, build directory, TMPDIR, rootfs, QEMU profile, and
  one-QEMU-at-a-time rule were retained.
- FLR-0078 p3 reached a running guest and launched a self-made native fixture,
  but its current QMP candidate was black. The harness QMP quit then timed out.
- p4 and p5 each reached QMP socket readiness but did not expose guest SSH or
  serial readiness. Their raw `runqemu` start logs and empty serial logs remain
  under `$QEMU_EVIDENCE_ROOT/flr0078/p4` and `p5`.
- Exact QEMU/runqemu processes were terminated only after re-reading their
  command identity. Post-cleanup checks reported no QEMU, runqemu, or QMP
  socket. No cache, image, source, Podman state, or TMPDIR was removed.

## Inferences

- A QMP greeting is only a host-side control-plane gate; it is not guest boot
  readiness. A bounded SSH protocol-banner check is the minimum next gate for
  the guest-SSH app-launch path.
- The QMP teardown timeout was caused by the harness sending LF while the
  capture client sends CRLF. The corrected harness must use one framing rule.

## Hypotheses / UNKNOWN

1. CRLF framing will make negotiated QMP `quit` deterministic on a responsive
   QEMU. This is falsifiable by a live start/quit check.
2. The p4/p5 guest boot stall is separate from QMP framing. UNKNOWN: whether it
   is a transient QEMU boot condition, an image service condition, or a serial
   observation gap. Do not classify it as a Flutter failure.
